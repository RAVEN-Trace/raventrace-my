# RAVEN Public Language Standard V1

**Status:** Editorial execution standard for public-facing RAVEN-Trace pages.  
**Canon:** Does not replace Raven Master Blueprint V3.0 or Reporter Domain Addendum V3.1.

## Core rule

Technical underneath. Simple on top.

RAVEN-Trace must preserve evidence discipline while making the public explanation understandable to a teenager, a casual reader and an older reader without specialist knowledge.

## Public-language tests

Every visible paragraph should pass five checks:

1. **Fact-safe** — factual assertions remain supported and uncertainty is visible.
2. **Easy** — a reader should understand the main point on one pass.
3. **Human** — wording should sound natural in Malaysian Malay, not like a legal memo or internal analyst note.
4. **Precise** — simplification must not change legal, financial or evidentiary meaning.
5. **Readable** — the sentence should give the reader a reason to continue.

## Default public rhythm

Use this order when explaining a development:

**Apa berlaku → Kenapa penting → Apa buktinya → Apa yang belum pasti → Apa seterusnya**

This is the public-language form of Raven's reporter rhythm: Lead → Context → Evidence → Gap → Impact → Next.

## Sentence rules

- Prefer one main idea per sentence.
- Prefer short paragraphs of 1–3 sentences.
- Put the plain-language meaning before technical detail.
- Explain acronyms and legal/financial terms on first use.
- Keep dates, amounts and status words exact.
- Use questions sparingly to create an explainer rhythm, not clickbait.
- Do not use dramatic verbs when the evidence only supports a process update.
- Do not imply guilt from arrest, remand, investigation or charge.

## Public labels

Use Malay-first labels in reader-facing copy:

- FACT → **FAKTA**
- CLAIM → **DAKWAAN**
- INFERENCE → **KESIMPULAN SEMENTARA**
- SPECULATION → **ANDAIAN**
- UNKNOWN → **BELUM DIKETAHUI**
- DISPUTED → **DIPERTIKAIKAN**
- Confidence → **Tahap keyakinan**
- Latest evidence snapshot → **Apa yang terbaru?**
- Executive briefing → **Ringkasan mudah**
- Case docket → **Status kes**
- Source room → **Sumber & dokumen**

Internal protocol terminology may remain in metadata, source IDs and audit layers where it adds value.

## Two-layer rule

### Layer 1 — public explanation

Lead with the clearest meaning in ordinary language.

### Layer 2 — audit detail

Then expose evidence labels, grades, source IDs, exact legal sections, technical accounting terms and unresolved gaps for readers who want to inspect deeper.

## Examples

Instead of:

> Masalah institusi direkodkan. Liabiliti jenayah individu masih perlu dibuktikan.

Use:

> RCI memang menemui masalah dalam cara Tabung Haji diurus. Tetapi siapa yang melakukan kesalahan jenayah masih perlu dibuktikan di mahkamah.

Instead of:

> Claim → checkpoint → verification

Use:

> Dakwaan → kami semak → apa yang terbukti

Instead of:

> Pembebasan daripada reman bukan NFA, acquittal atau penentuan bahawa seseorang tidak bersalah.

Use:

> Dilepaskan daripada reman tidak bermaksud kes sudah ditutup atau seseorang telah dibersihkan daripada semua siasatan. Status seterusnya perlu dilihat pada rekod rasmi.

## Tone

RAVEN-Trace should feel factual, calm and curious. "Fun" means easy and satisfying to read — not joking about serious allegations, victims, court proceedings or public harm.

Use memorable contrast where useful:

> 14 pelaburan disyor diperiksa ≠ 14 kes jenayah terbukti.

## Final editorial gate

Before publishing, ask:

- Can a 15-year-old explain the main point back correctly?
- Can an older non-specialist reader understand the key legal/process distinction?
- Is every strong-sounding word supported by evidence?
- Are unknowns visible without overwhelming the story?
- Can a reader reach the source or deeper evidence if they want to verify it?
